# Messaging for our Ideal Participants

Last January, I started Chris Kyle's [Launch Academy](https://www.chriskyle.com/launch-academy) program with the intent of using it as the framework for launching an OZR (Optimal Zone Resilience) program. That didn't happen, but there is much in the framework that can be useful for launching Essential Capacities.

For us now, I'd like to build on his marketing plan and his messaging blueprint.

## The webinar

His marketing approach is built around offering a free webinar that provides meaningful value along the lines of the program *and* encourages webinar participants to register for the program. It's more than an infomercial but it still has a marketing purpose. Typically, this is offered about two weeks before the program starts. We could do ours around mid-February.

He would focus all of the outreach pre-webinar on getting people to the webinar. With our use of Early-Bird registration, my sense is that before Jan 17, our outreach can encourage webinar sign-ups for everyone and Early-Bird registrations for those who are ready and would like the discount. We can highlight our money-back guarantee, so there is little risk for people who do Early-Bird. That said, my experience is that very few avail themselves of the money-back guarantee.

This means we need to define the webinar well enough for people to start registering for it in early January. We may want to create a webinar landing page that can be transitioned later into the program landing page. Webinar registration can happen through a Zoom page. We may want to upgrade the [current program registration page](https://www.context.org/engage/registration-for-essential-capacities/) to better match the program landing page.

Post-webinar, the focus shifts to actual program registrations, with outreach especially to those who registered for the webinar, whether they came or not. We can also continue via things like LinkedIn and Substack although with an expectation of lower response rate than those who came to the webinar.


## Messaging to our Ideal Participants

Chris Kyle has what he calls a four-component Course Development Framework:

* CDF Component #1:  Identify the Ideal Participant, or “Avatar”, for Your Course
* CDF Component #2:  Identify the Transformation that Participants Can Experience with Your Content
* CDF Component #3:  Create Your Authentic Messaging Blueprint
* CDF Component #4:  Design Your Step-by-Step Transformative Experience (Your Course Outline)

Here I'd like to focus on the first three components. We already have a good start on #4.

### Identifying the Ideal Participant 

Let's think of our "ideal participants" as a territory, not a simple category. We need to develop a shared, useful map for that territory that we can use ourselves and use with our AI assistants.

In addition, for strategic purposes, we want participants who will contribute in various ways back into the CI/BFN system once they have graduated. Our major constraint now is that we have a lot more good, meaningful opportunities and things to do than people to do them.

### Create Your Authentic Messaging Blueprint 

Chris offers the following framework (adapted with some BFN terminology):

* Identify the **Aspirations** or **Desires** of your ideal participant in the context of your program's territories
* Describe their **Pain/Problem/Challenge** (relating to their reality)
* Provide your **Diagnosis** for WHY this pain or problem or challenge is happening
* Offer a step-by-step **Solution** that helps the participants move out of their pain
* Your **Promise** for the participant’s experience coming out of the course
* Point to the **Desired Future-State** – the possible transformation from A to B.

I don't feel we need to follow this exactly, but there’s psychodynamic savvy in starting with aspirations and then offering a way to overcome at least some of the obstacles in the way of those aspirations.

Chris uses the exercise of writing up a "Dear Avatar" letter to your ideal participant that devotes a sequence of paragraphs to the messaging blueprint steps. It might be worthwhile doing something like that as a step towards the Substack and LinkedIn posts, and guidance for the landing page.
